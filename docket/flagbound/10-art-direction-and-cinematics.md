# 10 — Art Direction & Cinematics

## Direction

From the brief: prioritise **creativity, scale and exciting environments** over
photorealism. Players should feel like they are inside an epic adventure.

**Style target:** stylised fantasy realism — bold silhouettes, saturated
lighting, exaggerated scale, readable at speed. Think illustrated epic rather
than rendered documentary.

**Why stylised is the correct choice here, not just the cheaper one:**

1. **Readability.** Three environments, four classes, two teams, constant
   transformation — a photoreal treatment would make all of it harder to parse
   at a glance, especially for younger players.
2. **Age-appropriateness.** Stylised fantasy combat reads as adventure; realism
   pushes the same actions toward violence.
3. **Scale.** The collapse and ascent need to be *huge*. Stylisation lets scale
   be exaggerated for drama without the uncanny cost.
4. **Longevity.** Stylised art ages far better than realism chasing hardware.

## The three worlds

Each act must be recognisable from a single frame. The palettes are deliberately
opposed.

### Act I — Surface

- **Palette:** daylight — greens, warm stone, blue sky
- **Mood:** grand, open, hopeful. A classic fantasy siege at its peak
- **Landmarks:** two castle bases, high walls, watchtowers, a central bridge
- **Light:** natural sun, long shadows, clear visibility
- **Sound:** open air, wind through forest, distant battle

### Act II — Underground

- **Palette:** darkness cut by fire and glow — deep blues, black rock, orange
  lava, cyan crystal
- **Mood:** ancient, hidden, awe-inspiring. This place is *older* than the war
  above it
- **Landmarks:** a ruined underground city, lava channels, a great river cavern,
  crystal formations
- **Light:** dramatic and directional. Darkness that hides things, light that
  reveals routes
- **Sound:** close and echoing, dripping water, deep hollow ambience

### Act III — Sky

- **Palette:** brilliant and airy — white cloud, gold light, pale stone
- **Mood:** exhilarating and precarious. Nothing below you
- **Landmarks:** the risen towers, floating platforms, long thin bridges
- **Light:** blinding sun above the cloud layer — the visual reward for the
  darkness of Act II
- **Sound:** thin air, howling wind, the hum of ancient machinery

## Cinematic moments

The brief names the moments that matter and asks for memorable, shareable
spectacle. These are the four:

### 1. The ground breaks apart

The single most important visual in the game — it is the moment a player
realises Flagbound is not a normal CTF game, and it happens in their **first
match**, six minutes in.

Beats: tremor → dust rising → fracture light tracing the ground → castles
shearing → the whole battlefield hinging open → the fall, with the surface
receding above.

Cameras stay in the player's control. The player is *in* it, not watching it.

### 2. The underground reveal

The moment of landing: the cavern opens out and the scale of the hidden kingdom
becomes visible for the first time. Light sources placed to draw the eye across
the space so the player reads the size of it in one second.

### 3. The towers launch

Ancient machinery ignites, glyphs run along the ruins, and the structures tear
free of the earth and climb. The player rides one up. Breaking through the cloud
layer into full sunlight is the peak of the match's visual arc.

### 4. The final-second capture

Not authored geometry — an authored *response*. When a capture lands in the last
few seconds: time dilation, camera push, sound drop-out and swell, and an
automatically saved highlight clip.

## Shareability

The brief wants players to want to share these moments.

*Proposed:* automatic highlight capture for a defined set of triggers — final-30
captures, captures during a transition, long escort runs that succeed, and
first-discovery route runs. Clips are short, auto-trimmed, and exportable.

## Interface

- Minimal HUD — health, stamina, ability cooldowns, flag status, timer, score
- Flag status is always visible; it is the only thing that decides the match
- **A prominent phase timer** so the transitions are anticipated, not ambushing
- F.C.S. callouts as unobtrusive lower-third text with subtitles
- Team colours consistent and colour-blind-safe across all three environments —
  a colour that reads in daylight may vanish against lava or cloud, so team
  identification must be tested per act

## Audio

- **Music per act**, with the transitions scored as the crescendos they are
- Layered adaptive score — intensity follows flag state and proximity to the
  final whistle
- The three worlds have completely distinct sound beds; a player should know
  which act they are in with their eyes closed
- **F.C.S. always wins the mix.** If a warning cannot be heard over the collapse,
  the warning is useless
- Distinct, loud audio identity for every hazard — lava, wind, unstable
  platforms, incoming debris
