# 09 — Progression & Rewards

## The hard rule

From the brief: **progression focuses on skill and dedication rather than
allowing players to buy unfair advantages. A new player should still be able to
defeat experienced players through smart decisions and teamwork.**

This is a constraint on the entire economy, and it settles most design arguments
before they start:

> **Nothing that can be unlocked, purchased, or grinded may change a player's
> combat power, movement speed, health, ability cooldowns, or information
> access.**

A day-one player and a 500-hour player have identical stats. The difference
between them is knowledge and skill, and that is the only difference the game
sells.

## What is unlockable

From the brief: cosmetic rewards, armour designs, character customisation,
achievements, new abilities and special items.

| Category | Examples | Affects gameplay? |
|---|---|---|
| **Armour designs** | Sets, pieces, materials, weathering | No |
| **Character customisation** | Body, face, colours, banners, emblems | No |
| **Weapon skins** | Visual variants per class weapon | No |
| **Effects** | Ability VFX colours, capture flourishes, trails | No |
| **Emotes & callouts** | Celebrations, custom ping voice lines | No |
| **Titles & badges** | Earned from achievements | No |
| **Achievements** | Progress records across all systems | No |
| **F.C.S. voices** | Alternate assistant voice packs | No |

### "New abilities" — the careful one

The brief lists new abilities as an unlockable, and this is the only place the
brief's wording could collide with its own fairness rule. The resolution:

> Unlockable abilities are **sidegrades, not upgrades**. Every class ships with a
> complete, competitive ability kit on day one. Unlocked abilities are
> *alternatives* with different trade-offs, never strictly better versions.

Example: a Swiftblade starts with a dash. They might later unlock a
grappling-hook alternative — different range, different cooldown, different
skill floor, **same power budget**. A player who never unlocks anything is never
outgunned; they just have fewer styles available.

Every new ability must be balanced against the starter kit before it ships, and
its win-rate delta measured after. If an unlocked ability outperforms the
starter option, that is a launch-blocking bug, not a monetisation success.

## How progression is earned

**Earned from contribution, not eliminations** — consistent with
[04](04-teamwork-scoring-and-strategies.md). A defensive player and an
aggressive player progress at the same rate.

Sources:

- **Match completion** — a baseline award; finishing matters, so leaving does not
- **Contribution score** — the main driver
- **Objectives** — captures, defences, returns, escorts, discoveries
- **Achievements** — one-time milestones across all play styles
- **Discovery** — finding hidden areas across the underground and sky phases
- **Challenges** — rotating goals, *proposed:* daily and weekly, deliberately
  spread across roles so they teach breadth rather than encourage stat-farming

**Challenge design rule:** no challenge may reward behaviour that hurts the
team. "Get 10 eliminations" makes players abandon the flag. "Escort a carrier to
a successful capture" does not. Challenges must always point at the objective.

**Losing still progresses.** *Proposed:* a loss awards meaningfully less than a
win but never nothing. A 10-year-old on a losing streak needs a reason to queue
again.

## Monetisation posture

*Proposed, and consistent with the brief's fairness requirement:*

- Cosmetics only. No power, no stats, no information.
- No loot boxes or randomised paid rewards — a poor fit for a game aimed at
  under-13s and increasingly restricted by regulation.
- Direct purchase where the player sees exactly what they are buying.
- Everything gameplay-relevant is free, permanently.
- No pay-to-skip on anything competitive.

The final monetisation model is a business decision that sits outside this
docket. The constraint that it cannot touch competitive balance does not.

## Anti-frustration

- No mandatory grind gates on classes — all four are available immediately.
- No stamina/energy systems limiting how much a player can play.
- Cosmetic unlocks visible and trackable, so effort has a destination.
- *Proposed:* no seasonal expiry on earned (as opposed to purchased) rewards —
  a badge earned in season 1 stays earned.

## Achievements as a teaching tool

Achievements are the quiet tutorial for the game's depth. A good spread teaches
players that support play exists:

- **First Steal** — capture a flag
- **Unbroken Line** — defend your base ten times in one match
- **Pathfinder** — discover 20 hidden routes
- **Shieldmate** — escort carriers to 50 successful captures
- **Groundbreaker** — capture a flag during a phase transition
- **Skybound** — capture in the final 10 seconds of the sky phase
- **Adaptive** — win a match after being behind at the ascent
- **Deep Explorer** — find every hidden area in the underground kingdom
